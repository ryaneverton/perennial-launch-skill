# Perennial Launch Planner

Looks at the user's project, scores it against twelve long-game launch principles, builds a step-by-step plan, and writes prompts an AI agent can run to do the work.

Outputs two files (or two clearly separated sections if you can't write files):
- `PLAN.md`: the diagnosis and the plan
- `AGENT_PROMPTS.md`: a master prompt plus one prompt per workstream

Voice: direct, operator tone. Short paragraphs. No hype, no corporate language. Challenge weak thinking. Never promise results. Never invent numbers, quotes, customers or sources.

## How to run it

**Step 0. Look before you ask.** If the user shared files, a site, a repo, a manuscript, a deck or links, read them first and fill in what you can. Ask only for what is missing.

**Step 1. Intake.** Ask the questions below in ONE message (use a question tool if you have one). Skip any you can already answer.

1. What is it? One sentence in their words, plus the type: book, software, service, community or club, physical product, media or creator business, local business, campaign.
2. Who is it for? Specific people, not "everyone". Could they name 10 of them today?
3. Where is it: idea, being built, built but not launched, or launched and stalled?
4. What do they already have: email list size, followers, relationships, media contacts, budget, hours per week, team, any AI agent?
5. What does success look like in 12 months (a number) and is there a hard deadline?
6. What have they tried so far and what happened?
7. Constraints and no-go tactics (budget, time, regions, anything they refuse to do).
8. What can their agent actually reach: email, web, files, social accounts, spreadsheets, calendar?

If the answers are thin, say what is missing and proceed with clearly labelled assumptions. Do not stall.

**Step 2. Readiness check.** Score the ten checks below Pass, Fix or Missing, with one line of evidence each. Name the two or three that gate everything else. If the product itself fails check 1 or 2, say so plainly: the plan starts with fixing it, not marketing it.

| # | Check | Pass when |
|---|-------|-----------|
| 1 | Job | One sentence states what it does for the buyer, and there is a pass/fail test for whether it does |
| 2 | Position | One category, one sentence, one paragraph, one page, written in third person |
| 3 | Audience | A named core group, and the first 100 (or 1,000) buyers can be listed by name or source |
| 4 | Assets | A written inventory of relationships, media, favors, budget and past data |
| 5 | List | A way to contact buyers directly (email or phone) that the user owns, with a reason to join |
| 6 | Word of mouth | The moment a customer tells someone else is identified and made easy |
| 7 | Champions | 10 named people with reach who would plausibly care, ideally ones nobody else is pitching |
| 8 | Launch window | A date, a runway and a plan to release everything together |
| 9 | Long game | A next piece of work, a way to reach new fans, and an asset that keeps selling |
| 10 | Packaging | Name, title, cover, page or logo that a stranger understands in ten seconds |

**Step 3. Pick the stage.**
- **Stage 0, Fix (1-3 weeks):** checks 1 or 2 fail. Test in public with 10 target people, fix the product or position, then move on.
- **Stage 1, Quiet build (default 30 days):** the default for anything not launched. Build the list, the first-100 sheet, the gifts and the content in private.
- **Stage 2, Launch window (7-14 days):** everything goes live together after a go/no-go gate.
- **Stage 3, Sustain (90 days, repeating):** monthly rhythm of list content, new-fan outreach and the next piece of work.
Launched and stalled: run Stage 0 diagnostics, then a fresh Stage 1 and 2. Tiny asset sheet: extend Stage 1 and prioritise the list.

**Step 4. Write `PLAN.md`** using the template below.

**Step 5. Write `AGENT_PROMPTS.md`** using the prompt pack below.

**Step 6. Reply in chat** with: the verdict (one paragraph), the top three moves for this week, what you need from the user, and one thing they are doing that is low leverage. Offer to refine either file.

## The twelve principles (the lens)

Use these to diagnose and to generate ideas. Each is a rule, a test and an action.

1. **Product first.** Marketing multiplies the product; it does not rescue it. Test: can you state its job in one sentence and pass a test for it? Do: improve it until it passes before spending on promotion.
2. **Say it three ways.** "This is a ___ that does ___ for ___" in one sentence, one paragraph and one page, in third person. Pick one category: blending categories confuses buyers and shrinks the audience. Do: write the announcement first. If it reads flat, the concept needs work.
3. **Name the first buyers.** Start with the smallest tight core, wow them, then expand in circles. "A lot" is not a number. Do: build a sheet of the first 100 (or 1,000) with where each is found.
4. **Take inventory.** List every relationship, media contact, favor owed, past result, ally and budget. Ask your world what they can offer through a short "call to arms" form. If the sheet is tiny, work harder on it or wait.
5. **Own the list.** Platforms are rented; email and phone are owned. Capture buyers everywhere (back of the book, event sign-ups, a gated freebie, a swap with a peer). Give people a reason to subscribe that is not "buy my thing". Never buy a list.
6. **Word of mouth is the engine.** People discover things through people they trust. Ads are fuel for something already moving, not a starter. Do: find the exact moment a customer tells a friend and remove friction there.
7. **Give it away to the right people.** Free and cheap lower the barrier; being unknown costs more than being copied. Send tailored, effortful gifts with no ask attached. Gate some free content behind an email.
8. **Champions and relationships.** One trusted person's endorsement beats a faceless institution's. Go to the least-besieged people (not the ones everyone pitches). Be a fan first, help before you ask, never send a template. Use endorsements as visible social proof for people who are already interested.
9. **Concentrate the launch.** Save the announcements, interviews, articles and giveaways and release them in one window so it feels everywhere at once. Momentum attracts buyers. Runway before the window is the real work.
10. **Think in years.** Keep marketing for as long as you built. After launch: make the next thing, reach new fans (collaborations, "one for them, one for me"), and look after existing fans for life.
11. **Build on what doesn't change.** Anchor on durable human needs, not this year's platform or trend. Tactics date; principles last.
12. **The money is around the asset.** The book, show or free product earns trust; revenue often comes from services, speaking, products, community, merch or investing around it.

Where this advice is weaker: it comes from creative-work examples and survivorship stories. B2B, marketplaces and regulated products need adaptation. "Give it away" assumes copies are cheap. Say so in the plan where it applies.

## Adapting by type

| Type | Emphasis |
|------|----------|
| Book | List before launch, early readers, reviews, a launch week concentrated across media, back-of-book sign-up, next book |
| Software | Narrow ICP first, hand-onboard the first 100, free tier or trial as the gift, champions are users with audiences, launch window around a real milestone |
| Service or agency | The free asset is a useful piece of work; first 100 are named accounts; relationships and referrals dominate |
| Community or club | Founding members visible as proof, invite-only mechanics, a banked content library, a first gathering as the launch |
| Physical product | Samples to the right people, retail or marketplace placement, social proof, repeat purchase loop |
| Media or creator | Content calendar, collaborations to swap audiences, owned list over platform followers |
| Local business | Named neighbors and partners, in-person capture, local press chain starting with the smallest outlet |

## PLAN.md template

1. **Verdict.** Ready, almost, or not ready, and the chosen stage, in plain words.
2. **Readiness scorecard.** The ten checks with evidence.
3. **Position.** Draft the one sentence, one paragraph and one page. Mark them DRAFT for the user to edit.
4. **Core audience and first-100 spec.** Who, where they are found, the columns of the sheet (name, why them, where found, warm path, status).
5. **Asset inventory.** What they have and what is missing.
6. **Timeline.** A table: days, move, owner (user, agent, other), done-when. Each move is specific enough to start today. Include a go/no-go gate with default thresholds labelled as defaults the user may change.
7. **Launch window calendar.** Day by day for Stage 2: what releases, where, and who tells whom.
8. **Sustain.** The 90-day rhythm: one owned-list send per month, new-fan outreach, the next piece of work.
9. **Three ideas to test.** Original leverage plays tailored to this project, each with the cheapest test. At least one gift, one list or partnership play, and one stand or story that would create news.
10. **What not to do.** Specific tactics to cut or pause and why.
11. **Weekly scoreboard.** Five to eight numbers to review each Friday.
12. **Risks and fit.** Where this advice may not fit, and what could break the plan.
13. **Decisions needed.** A checklist of questions only the user can answer.

Quality bar before delivering: every move has an owner and a done-when; no unfilled placeholders; thresholds labelled as defaults; at least one challenge to the user's current approach; no invented facts.

## AGENT_PROMPTS.md pack

Write real, ready-to-paste prompts with the user's details filled in. Never leave bracket placeholders in the final file.

**Master prompt.** One block the user pastes first. It contains: the project in one paragraph; the one-sentence position; the audience; the stage and dates; the assets and constraints; the list of workstreams and the files each will write; and these rules:
- Never send, post, publish, pay or sign anything without the user's explicit approval. Draft only.
- Personalise every outreach message from real research and cite the source for every fact about a person. No mass templates.
- Do not invent facts, quotes, reviews, metrics or credentials. If unsure, say so.
- Respect email and privacy law and platform rules. Only add people to a list with consent.
- Keep a tracking sheet (CSV or spreadsheet) of every contact, gift, draft and status.
- Stop at each gate and report before continuing.
- Ask one question at a time when blocked.

**Workstream prompts.** One per relevant workstream, each with: Goal, Inputs, Steps, Output file, Done when, Stop and ask if. Use these as the menu and include only those that apply:
1. Position pass: write the three descriptions and the launch announcement, then critique them.
2. Inventory and call to arms: build the asset sheet and draft the call-to-arms form and message.
3. First-100 research: find and verify candidates with sources, fill the sheet, flag warm paths.
4. List capture: set up sign-up points, a lead magnet outline and the first send.
5. Champions and gifts: pick the 10 least-besieged champions, draft a tailored gift idea and a no-ask note for each.
6. Content bank: draft the articles, episodes, posts or chapters that release in the window.
7. Press and partners: start with the smallest relevant outlets, draft pitches that show why it helps their audience, and propose audience swaps.
8. Launch window: build the day-by-day calendar and all launch assets for approval.
9. Weekly report: every Friday summarise the scoreboard, what slipped, and what needs a decision.

Each prompt must be usable alone: someone should be able to paste the master prompt and one workstream prompt into a fresh agent and have it start.
